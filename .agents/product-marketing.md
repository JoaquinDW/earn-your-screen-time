# Product Marketing Context

**Document version:** v1
**Last updated:** 2026-10-01

## Product Overview

**One-liner:** Earnit is an iPhone app that makes distracting-app access something you earn through movement or focused work.

**What it does:** People choose the apps that pull them off course; Earnit keeps those apps shielded by default. Walking steps from Apple Health, a verified focused study activity, or phone-counted push-ups earn screen-time minutes, which can be spent in deliberate 5-, 10-, or 15-minute sessions. When a session ends, the shield returns automatically.

**Product category:** Earn screen time / app blocker / digital-detox and focus app.

**Product type:** Consumer iOS mobile app (B2C), using Apple Health and iOS Screen Time / Family Controls.

**Business model:** Subscription-only product, with monthly and yearly auto-renewing plans and a three-day free trial when eligible. There is no permanent free tier; an active subscription unlocks app blocking and time earning. Current US pricing anchors documented for the Store listing are $4.99/month and $39.99/year, with localized App Store pricing.

## Target Audience

**Target companies:** Not applicable; individual iPhone users.

**Decision-makers:** The user themself. Parents buying or setting up a device are not the primary audience in current positioning.

**Primary use case:** Replace reactive attempts to stop doomscrolling with a clear trade: do a meaningful action first, then take a short, intentional screen-time session.

**Jobs to be done:**
- Help me stop opening the apps that consume my day on autopilot without banning them forever.
- Give me a concrete reason to walk, study, or do a quick set of push-ups.
- Let me use social apps deliberately, in short bounded windows, rather than negotiate with myself each time.

**Use cases:**
- A person who wants Instagram, TikTok, or similar distraction apps unavailable until they move.
- A student who needs the phone out of reach until a focused study task is complete.
- Someone who wants a low-friction physical prompt (steps or push-ups) before returning to entertainment apps.
- Users seeking a gentler digital-detox habit: progress and visibility without guilt, streak-shaming, or red-warning framing.

## Personas

Not applicable as a buying-committee model: Earnit is an individual consumer purchase. Initial audience hypotheses to validate are students, habitual social-media scrollers, and people who want movement-linked phone boundaries.

## Problems & Pain Points

**Core problem:** Distracting apps are immediately available at the exact moment a person is trying to focus, study, move more, or break automatic scrolling.

**Why alternatives fall short:** Timers and limits ask a person to stop while they are already in the moment of temptation, leaving an easy extension, snooze, or internal negotiation. A total block can feel punitive or too rigid. Generic fitness trackers do not provide a direct payoff for effort, while ordinary app blockers do not create a constructive route back to access.

**What it costs them:** Lost attention, delayed study/work, less movement, unplanned time in social apps, and less confidence that their boundaries will hold.

**Emotional tension:** “I know I should stop, but I keep overriding myself.” The user wants agency and relief, not shame or a permanent ban.

## Competitive Landscape

**Direct:** Unrot: Earn your Screen Time; Blocus: Earn Screen Time; Piggy, Koia, DoomIt, and Peep — these address the same earn-access concept. Earnit differentiates through multiple earning paths (walking, study, and on-device push-up counting), explicit short sessions, and a calm, non-shaming framing. This is a positioning hypothesis, not a feature-by-feature verified comparison.

**Secondary:** Opal, ScreenZen, BePresent, and conventional app blockers / screen-time limiters — they help curb app usage, but are positioned around limits or blocks rather than an effort-for-access exchange.

**Indirect:** iOS Screen Time timers, self-imposed rules, focus timers, fitness trackers, parental controls, and deleting apps — each can help, but does not combine default blocking with a personally earned, intentional access window.

## Differentiation

**Key differentiators:**
- The default is protected access: selected apps stay paused until the user earns and spends time.
- Earnable access comes from three distinct actions: Apple Health steps, a focused study flow, and live on-device push-up counting.
- Access is constrained to an explicit 5-, 10-, or 15-minute session; the block restores automatically.
- Product design frames the behavior as a choice and a trade, not punishment; it explicitly avoids guilt and streak-shaming.
- Privacy is central: health and motion data remain on the device; app-selection tokens are not visible to Earnit; push-up video, images, and body landmarks are not recorded, saved, or uploaded.

**How we do it differently:** Earnit moves the decision upstream: users do the effort, build a balance, and later choose a finite access session rather than deciding whether to stop while already scrolling.

**Why that's better:** It creates useful friction at the point of access while leaving a clear, achievable way through. The experience preserves autonomy better than an outright ban and provides an immediate reason to move or focus.

**Why customers choose us:** They want to “earn screen time” rather than merely track it or be told to stop, and value flexible effort options plus strict, automatic session boundaries.

## Objections

| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| “I can just override a screen-time limit.” | Earnit’s value is default shielding plus an explicit earned balance and bounded access session, reducing the in-the-moment negotiation common with timers. |
| “A full block sounds too restrictive.” | It is not a permanent ban: users select the apps and earn intentional 5/10/15-minute access windows. |
| “I do not want my health, camera, or app data collected.” | Steps use Apple Health; motion/health data stay on-device; selected apps remain private; push-up counting does not record, save, or upload video, images, or body landmarks. |
| “Why pay for this?” | The subscription supports the core blocking-and-earning system, with a three-day trial when eligible. Validate price/value fit with user research rather than overclaiming ROI. |

**Anti-persona:** People seeking a conventional free step counter, a passive usage report, parental monitoring/control, a permanent app ban, Android support, or a one-time-purchase model.

## Switching Dynamics

**Push:** Frustration with doomscrolling, easily bypassed timers, lost focus, and movement/study goals that do not stick.

**Pull:** A simple, tangible promise: “move first, scroll later”; multiple ways to earn; a route to access that is earned rather than forbidden.

**Habit:** Instant app access, established social-media reflexes, default iOS limits, and the convenience of doing nothing differently.

**Anxiety:** Fear that blocking will be too strict, the system will be annoying to maintain, it will not work with selected apps, or sensitive health/camera/app information will be exposed.

## Brand Voice

**Tone:** Direct, calm, encouraging, and grounded — never scolding, overly cheerful, or gamified-shouty.

**Style:** Short, second-person language; concrete actions; confident boundaries; simple explanations of privacy and control. The product can be candid (“Your phone shouldn't be free”) but should always pair the boundary with an achievable path forward.

**Personality:** Intentional, practical, empathetic, disciplined, and quietly motivating.

## Proof Points

**Metrics:** Product mechanics that can be demonstrated: users earn five minutes for every 500 steps in the current default paywall message; sessions are 5/10/15 minutes; a personalized 30-day journey shows earning/spending patterns. These are product facts, not outcome metrics.

## Goals

**Business goal:** Grow a paid iOS subscription product in the emerging “earn screen time” category, initially by winning high-intent, lower-competition App Store search terms and converting qualified visitors into trial users and subscribers.

**Conversion action:** Start the three-day free trial / subscribe after configuring selected apps and seeing the personalized plan.

**Current metrics:** The ASO research notes few installs. No acquisition, activation, trial, conversion, retention, or cancellation metrics are documented in the repository. Establish a baseline before making outcome claims.
